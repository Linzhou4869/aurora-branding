# Diabetes Prevention Workshop
## Patient Education Script (Revised for Accessibility)

**Version:** Q4 2026  
**Reading Level:** 6th-8th grade  
**Languages Available:** English, Spanish, Vietnamese, Mandarin, Arabic  
**Format:** In-person and phone-friendly (no technology required)

---

## Welcome & Introduction (5 minutes)

### Script for Facilitator:

"Hello everyone, and welcome. My name is [NAME], and I'll be leading today's workshop.

**First, I want to say: you belong here.** This workshop is for anyone who wants to learn about preventing diabetes—whether you've been told you're 'pre-diabetic,' you have a family history, or you just want to stay healthy.

**Before we start, a few important things:**

- **Language support:** If English is not your first language, please raise your hand. We have interpreters available in Spanish, Vietnamese, Mandarin, and Arabic. You should have been offered an interpreter when you signed up. If not, we'll get you one right now—please don't worry about this.

- **No technology needed:** You don't need a phone, app, or computer for today. Everything we share will be given to you on paper. If you want to follow along on a device, that's fine too—but it's not required.

- **Questions are welcome:** There are no 'silly' questions. If something doesn't make sense, please stop me. Chances are, someone else is wondering the same thing.

- **This is not a test:** You don't need to remember everything. We'll give you a simple handout to take home with the key points.

**Today, we'll cover three things:**
1. What is diabetes, and why does it matter?
2. What can you actually do to prevent it?
3. How can we support you—because you don't have to do this alone.

We'll take a break halfway through. Feel free to step out anytime if you need to use the restroom or take a call."

## Part 1: What Is Diabetes? (10 minutes)

### Script for Facilitator:

"Let's start with the basics. What is diabetes?

**Here's the simple version:**

Your body runs on sugar (called 'glucose'). It's like fuel for a car. Every time you eat, your body breaks down food into sugar, and that sugar goes into your blood.

Now, your body has a helper called **insulin**. Think of insulin like a key. Its job is to unlock your cells so the sugar can get inside and give you energy.

**In diabetes, this system breaks down:**

- **Type 1 diabetes:** Your body stops making insulin. (This usually starts in childhood and is not preventable.)

- **Type 2 diabetes:** Your body still makes insulin, but it doesn't work well. This is called 'insulin resistance.' The key doesn't fit the lock anymore. Sugar stays in your blood instead of going into your cells.

**Why does this matter?**

When sugar stays in your blood for too long, it causes damage. Over time, high blood sugar can lead to:
- Heart problems
- Vision loss
- Kidney damage
- Nerve damage (especially in feet and hands)
- Slow healing of cuts and wounds

**The good news:** Type 2 diabetes can often be prevented or delayed. That's what we're here to talk about today."

### Visual Aid Suggestion:
*Simple diagram showing: Food → Sugar in Blood → Insulin (Key) → Cells (Lock) → Energy*

### Check for Understanding:

"Before we move on, let me check: Does this make sense so far? Thumbs up if it's clear, thumbs sideways if you're still thinking about it, thumbs down if you're confused and need me to explain again."

*[Pause for responses. Address any confusion before continuing.]*

## Part 2: What Increases Your Risk? (10 minutes)

### Script for Facilitator:

"Now, you might be wondering: 'Why me? What caused this?'

**Here's the truth: Diabetes is not your fault.**

Some things you can't control. Some things you can. Let's talk about both.

### Things You CAN'T Control:

- **Family history:** If your parents or siblings have diabetes, your risk is higher. This is not something you chose.

- **Age:** Risk goes up after age 45. But we're seeing it in younger people now too.

- **Ethnicity:** Some groups have higher risk: African American, Hispanic/Latino, Native American, Asian American, Pacific Islander. This is about genetics and also about access to healthy food and safe places to exercise—not anything you did wrong.

- **Past pregnancy:** If you had gestational diabetes during pregnancy, your risk is higher.

### Things You CAN Influence:

- **Weight:** Carrying extra weight, especially around your belly, increases risk. But you don't need to lose a lot—even 5-7% of your body weight makes a difference. (For a 200-pound person, that's just 10-14 pounds.)

- **Activity level:** Moving your body less than 3 times a week increases risk.

- **Blood pressure:** High blood pressure and diabetes often go together.

- **Cholesterol:** Unhealthy cholesterol levels increase risk.

**Important:** You don't need to change everything at once. Small steps count. We'll talk about what those steps look like."

### Check for Understanding:

"Does anyone want to share what you're hearing? What's one thing that surprised you?"

*[Invite 2-3 responses. Validate all contributions.]*

## Part 3: What Can You Do? (15 minutes)

### Script for Facilitator:

"Okay, let's get to the part you've been waiting for: **What can you actually do?**

I'm going to be honest with you. There's a lot of confusing information out there. 'Eat this, not that.' 'Do this exercise.' 'Buy this product.' It's overwhelming.

**Here's the good news: You don't need to do everything. You need to do a few things consistently.**

Research shows that three changes make the biggest difference:

---

### Change #1: Move Your Body More

**What the research says:** 150 minutes of activity per week cuts your diabetes risk by 58%.

**What this means in real life:**
- 30 minutes a day, 5 days a week
- OR 10 minutes, 3 times a day (if 30 minutes feels impossible)

**What counts as 'activity'?**
- Walking (yes, just walking!)
- Dancing in your living room
- Gardening
- Playing with your kids or grandkids
- Housework (vacuuming, mopping)
- Taking the stairs instead of the elevator

**What if I can't walk?**
- Chair exercises count
- Arm movements while sitting
- Stretching

**Start small:** If you're doing nothing now, start with 5 minutes. That's okay. Build from there.

---

### Change #2: Eat in a Way That Works for You

**What the research says:** Losing 5-7% of body weight prevents diabetes in most people.

**What this means in real life:**
- You don't need a perfect diet
- You don't need to cut out all your favorite foods
- You DO need to make some shifts

**Simple swaps that work:**

| Instead of... | Try... |
|---------------|--------|
| Sugary drinks (soda, juice) | Water, unsweetened tea |
| White bread, white rice | Whole wheat bread, brown rice |
| Large portions | Slightly smaller portions |
| Eating fast | Eating slowly, stopping when full |
| Skipping meals | Regular meals to avoid overeating later |

**What about cost?**
We hear you. Healthy food can be expensive. Here are some ideas:
- Frozen vegetables are just as healthy as fresh (and often cheaper)
- Buy in bulk when possible
- Look for sales and use coupons
- Community food programs may help—ask us about resources

**What about culture?**
Your traditional foods matter. You don't need to give them up. We can talk about how to include them in a healthy way. If you want, stay after and we can discuss your specific foods.

---

### Change #3: Get Support

**What the research says:** People who have support are more likely to succeed.

**What this means in real life:**
- You don't have to do this alone
- Tell your family what you're trying to do
- Find a friend who wants to walk with you
- Join a group (like this one!)
- Ask your doctor for resources

**We are here to help:**
- We can connect you with a health coach
- We have follow-up groups that meet monthly
- We can help with transportation if that's a barrier
- We have interpreter services if you prefer another language

---

### Check for Understanding:

"Of these three changes—moving more, eating differently, getting support—which one feels most doable for you right now? Take 30 seconds to think about it."

*[Pause. Invite a few volunteers to share.]*

## Part 4: Breaking Down Barriers (10 minutes)

### Script for Facilitator:

"I want to pause here and acknowledge something: **Knowing what to do is not the same as being able to do it.**

Life gets in the way. We know this. Let's talk about common barriers and what we can do about them.

### Common Barriers & Solutions:

| Barrier | What You Can Do | What WE Can Do |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|
| 'I don't have time' | Start with 5-10 minutes. Do it during lunch or after dinner. | We offer evening and weekend sessions. We can help you find time that works. |
| 'I can't afford healthy food' | Focus on one swap at a time. Frozen veggies, beans, eggs are affordable. | We can connect you with food assistance programs. Ask us. |
| 'I don't have a safe place to walk' | Walk inside (mall, community center, your home). Chair exercises work too. | We can share information about free community exercise programs. |
| 'My family eats differently' | Start with your own plate. You don't need to cook separate meals. | We can provide family-inclusive nutrition guidance. |
| 'I don't speak English well' | Ask for an interpreter. It's your right. | We provide interpreters in Spanish, Vietnamese, Mandarin, Arabic. Tell us what you need. |
| 'I don't have transportation' | Ask about telehealth options. Some programs offer home visits. | We can help with transportation vouchers. Ask at the front desk. |
| 'I tried before and failed' | This is not failure. It's information. Try again, differently. | We're here to support you long-term, not just today. |

**The point is:** Barriers are real. But they're not the end of the story. We want to work WITH you to find what's possible."

### Activity (Optional, 5 minutes):

"Turn to the person next to you. Share one barrier you're facing and one small step you could take this week. You have 3 minutes."

*[Facilitate pairs. Walk around and listen. Offer support where needed.]*

## Part 5: Your Action Plan (10 minutes)

### Script for Facilitator:

"Alright, let's make this real. I'm going to give you a simple worksheet. It has three questions:

1. **What is ONE thing I will do this week?** (Be specific: 'I will walk 10 minutes after dinner on Monday, Wednesday, Friday.')

2. **What might get in my way?** (Be honest: 'It might rain.' 'I might be tired.')

3. **How will I handle it?** ('If it rains, I'll walk inside.' 'If I'm tired, I'll just do 5 minutes.')

**This is called an 'if-then plan.'** Research shows people who make these plans are more likely to follow through.

*[Distribute worksheets. Give participants 5 minutes to write. Offer help to anyone who needs it.]*

**When you're done:**
- Keep this somewhere you'll see it (fridge, phone, wallet)
- Share it with someone who can support you
- Bring it to your next appointment—we'll check in on how it went

We are here to support you. Let's do this together."

## Appendix: Facilitator Notes

### Language Access Reminders:
- Confirm interpreter needs at registration AND 48 hours before workshop
- Have phone interpreter line ready as backup
- Provide all handouts in patient's preferred language
- Speak directly to patient, not to interpreter
- Allow extra time for interpreted sessions

### Technology-Free Options:
- All materials available in print
- Phone-based follow-up available (no app required)
- Paper check-in cards for patients without phones
- In-person registration (no online-only signup)

### Health Literacy Best Practices:
- Use simple words (avoid: "glucose," "metabolism," "cardiovascular")
- Explain numbers in context ("150 minutes" = "30 minutes, 5 days")
- Use visuals when possible
- Check understanding frequently (thumbs up/down, teach-back)
- Provide written summary for later reference

### Cultural Sensitivity:
- Acknowledge traditional foods (don't label as "bad")
- Recognize family dynamics (elders, children, decision-makers)
- Respect different beliefs about health and body
- Offer gender-specific groups if requested

### Common Questions & Suggested Responses:

| Question | Suggested Response |
|----------|-------------------|
| "Do I have to give up rice?" | "You don't have to give it up. Try smaller portions and add more vegetables. Brown rice is a healthier option if you like it." |
| "I work two jobs. I don't have time." | "Even 5 minutes counts. Can you park farther away and walk? Can you do stretches during breaks? Let's find what fits YOUR life." |
| "My family says I'm fine. Why change?" | "Your family cares about you. Sometimes we don't know we have a problem until it's serious. This is about staying healthy for them too." |
| "I've tried before. It didn't work." | "I hear you. What didn't work? Let's figure out what was hard and try something different. You're not starting from zero—you're starting from experience." |
